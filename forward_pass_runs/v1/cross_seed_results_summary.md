# Cross-Seed Results Summary

Across seeds 1, 2, and 42, the evidence profile is highly stable. In each seed, 27 of 40 variables passed the activation-recoverability screen, 33 passed the directional-consistency screen, 26 passed the split-generalization screen, and 17 reached the learned-direction viability stage. The final conservative criterion identified the same 12 variables as direction-viability candidates in all three seeds, indicating that the strongest effects are not artifacts of a single random initialization.

The 12 stable candidates were morphosyntactic alignment, voice and agent prominence, causativity and valency change, morphological segmentation type, agreement indexing density, definiteness and specificity, gender/noun class, inclusive/exclusive distinction, pronoun richness and reduction, mirativity/stance/affect marking, quantifier scope and distributivity, and quotation/reported-speech structure. Across these candidates, mean sentence-level activation AUROC was 0.858, with individual candidate means ranging from 0.744 to 0.998. Mean learned delta-direction AUROC was 0.956, suggesting that these variables are not merely classifiable from activations, but also show a stable direction of representation under the contrast-pair intervention.

Five variables consistently passed activation recoverability, directional consistency, and split generalization, but failed the learned-direction viability check: subject expression/pro-drop, case marking, transitivity/valency, analytic versus synthetic encoding, and topic-comment structure. These variables should be treated as activation-recoverable but not yet directionally interpretable. They may still be useful for broader representation analysis, but they do not currently support stronger mechanistic claims.

Thirteen variables consistently failed the initial activation-recoverability criterion: basic constituent order, nominal modifier order, optionality versus obligatoriness of marking, redundancy/cumulative exponence, number marking, aspect and event structure, modality and mood, evidentiality, subordination and embedding, discourse relation marking, social deixis/honorifics/status encoding, deixis and perspective anchoring, and orthographic/tokenization interface. These failures suggest either weak encoding in the tested activation site/model, insufficient dataset contrast strength, or heavy dependence on surface forms not captured by the current probe setup.

The remaining variables mainly showed overfit risk or unstable controls. Animacy/humanness, person marking/person hierarchy, possession/alienability, tense prominence, epistemic modality, negation/polarity structure, focus/given-new marking, and genericity/kind-level reference were overfit-risk cases in all three seeds. Conditional/counterfactual marking was overfit-risk in seeds 1 and 2 but failed the null-label control in seed 42. Speech-act force/request directness failed directional consistency in all three seeds. Overall, the cross-seed pattern supports a restricted but credible mechanistic follow-up: subsequent SAE analysis should prioritize the 12 stable direction-viability candidates, while treating the failed, overfit-risk, and directionally unstable variables as secondary diagnostic targets rather than primary claims.
